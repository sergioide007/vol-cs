# Foreword

Foundations—Volume 1—articulates how to engineer an agent. This volume investigates *where* and *against what adversary* that agent must survive.

The technological frontier is unforgiving. Adversaries are actively weaponizing autonomous agents against enterprise agent swarms; regulatory bodies are drafting sweeping mandates while underlying model capabilities mutate each quarter; and vendors across foundation models, silicon, and developer harnesses reshuffle the board continuously. Choosing an enterprise platform without understanding these forces does not save time: it accumulates existential strategic debt.

For this reason, every chapter in this tome alternates across three distinct lenses: the technical track, exposing internal mechanics and operational boundaries; the executive track, analyzing total cost of ownership, risk boundaries, and capital allocation; and the academic track, grounding claims in peer-reviewed empirical literature. If you lead an organization, read the second track; if you build systems, read the first; if you conduct research or teach, read the third. All three articulate the same truth at varying granularities.

The frontier moves relentlessly. The architectural criteria to navigate it—empirical verifiability, defense-in-depth, and sovereignty over your stack—are the permanent assets this volume instills.

**Sergio Perez Ruiz**  
*Lima, 2026*
